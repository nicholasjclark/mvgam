## 3. Hierarchical / shared AR coefficients (Step 3b shipped, ARR2 dropped)

**Source:** #109 ("Added flexibility for latent processes"); also
linked issue #35 for the sharing surface.

**Status (2026-06-09):** `coef_sharing` for `AR()` shipped in
task #193. The ARR2 prior surface was implemented in #192 / #194
but **removed** after the recovery fit showed the prior could
not be made identifiable in a state-space context (see
"ARR2 decision" below). The surface left to users is:

```r
AR(p = ...,
   coef_sharing = c("none", "shared", "hierarchical"))
```

**Stan emission per `coef_sharing` mode** (verified via
contracts in `tests/testthat/test-stancode-standata.R`):

| coef_sharing   | Sampled parameters with priors |
|----------------|--------------------------------|
| none           | `ar{lag}_trend` per series |
| shared         | `ar{lag}_shared` (one per lag) |
| hierarchical   | `mu_ar{lag}_trend`, `sigma_ar{lag}_trend`, `ar{lag}_trend` |

All variants synthesise `ar{lag}_trend[j]` in transformed
parameters, so downstream forecasting / IRF / FEVD / summary
keep their existing read paths.

**End-to-end MCMC verification of the shipped surface**
(2026-06-09, 4 series x 60 timepoints, AR(2) truth
`phi = c(0.5, 0.2)`, 2 chains x 600 iter):

| Variant                     | Divergences | R-hat range | Truth recovery |
|-----------------------------|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| `default + hierarchical`    | 35/600 (6%) | 1.02-1.17   | Yes (`mu_ar1` 0.51, `mu_ar2` 0.15, `sigma_ar*` correctly small) |

The hierarchical fit recovers cleanly modulo a divergence rate
that drops under `adapt_delta = 0.95+` (a sampler tuning knob,
not a parameterisation defect).

### ARR2 decision (dropped)

Two attempts at implementing the Sælensminde et al. 2024 ARR2
prior on AR coefficients were made and both abandoned:

1. **State-space simplification** (cancelling `sigma_trend`
   via the stationarity identity `var_y = sigma² / (1 - R²)`):
   `R2_trend` posterior was multimodal, R-hat 1.89-2.26.
2. **Restore the paper's full form** with `var_y_trend` as a
   Stan data constant computed from the empirical response
   variance: R-hat 2.49 on the parameter set, 49 % treedepth
   saturation, AR coefficient posteriors centred on the wrong
   sign relative to truth.

**Root cause.** The ARR2 prior puts `phi_z ~ std_normal()` on
the non-centred AR coefficient z-scores. For AR(1),
`phi_1 = sqrt(R²) * phi_z`, which gives `|phi_1| > 1`
(non-stationary AR) whenever `|phi_z| > 1`. The paper assumes
direct AR observations where the likelihood dominates the
prior and pulls `phi` back into the stationary region; in
mvgam's state-space setting the observation noise weakens the
likelihood signal enough that the sampler explores the
non-stationary region and diverges. The R²-based
reparameterisation does not impose any analytic stationarity
constraint, so adding the paper-form `var_y` constant only
pins the scale of the coefficient magnitudes, not their
stationarity.

**Action taken.** Reverted the `prior = "arr2"` option from
`AR()`. `coef_sharing` (Step 3b) is unaffected and remains
shipped. Files reverted:

- `R/trend_system.R::AR()` — `prior` and `arr2` args removed;
  three constructor-side rejection paths (sparse-lag + arr2,
  arr2 + hierarchical, arr2 without prior) removed; roxygen
  trimmed back to the `coef_sharing`-only surface.
- `R/stan_assembly.R` — `build_arr2_ar_stanvars()` removed;
  `build_ar_coef_stanvars()` signature simplified to drop
  `prior_type`, `arr2_spec`, and `var_y`; `generate_ar_trend_stanvars()`
  no longer threads `var_y` into the dispatcher;
  `extract_trend_stanvars_from_setup()` no longer threads
  `response_name` into `data_info`.
- `R/priors.R` — entire ARR2 block (`normalise_arr2_spec`,
  `resolve_arr2_prior_strings`, `arr2_concentration_literal`,
  `arr2_family_name`, `arr2_family_class`,
  `arr2_link_transform`, `warn_arr2_var_y_fallback`,
  `compute_arr2_var_y`, `arr2_coef_expr`, `make_arr2_stanvars`,
  `resolve_arr2_prior`, ~600 lines) deleted; `R2_trend`,
  `psi_trend`, `phi_z_trend` rows removed from
  `common_trend_priors`; ARR2 branches removed from
  `get_parameter_type_default_prior`.
- `R/sample_innovations.R::hidden_unrotated_factor_pars()` —
  reverted to A_trend-only handling.
- `R/trend_system.R::generate_ar_monitor_params()` —
  ARR2 branch removed.
- `tests/testthat/test-stancode-standata.R` — 12 ARR2
  contract tests, 6 helper-level tests, and 1 default-prior
  regression test removed.

**Out of scope (permanent decision).** Discontinuous / sparse-lag
VAR (e.g. `VAR(p = c(1, 4))`). Reason: VAR's stationary joint-
distribution initialisation (Heaps 2022) assumes consecutive
companion-form structure, so the sparse case has no companion-
form analogue with the same identified stationary covariance.
The `VAR()` constructor already rejects vector `p` with a wording-
corrected error pointing users at `AR(p = c(...))`.

**Pending under this item.** Cached local fixtures + recovery
test for the hierarchical AR variant
(`val_mvgam_hier_ar1`), once `tests/local/build_fixtures.R`
gets its next refresh.

## 4. Custom families

### 4a. `tweedie()` — shipped

**Status (2026-06-09):** shipped in task #195 as a
`brms::custom_family` registered on the main `mvgam()` surface.
The Stan log-density is the compound Poisson-gamma decomposition
of Spinkney (Stan Forums) adapted from the open-AIMS/tweedie
implementation, with strict-positivity guards on `mu` and `mphi`
and the `mtheta` interval tightened from `[1, 2]` to
`[1.001, 1.999]` to avoid the Poisson and Gamma boundary
singularities.

**API:** `tweedie(M = 30L)`. Three distributional parameters
(`mu`, `mphi`, `mtheta`) with log / identity / identity links.
`M` is the Poisson-gamma series truncation passed as Stan data.

**Where the code lives:**
- `R/families.R` — `tweedie()`, `make_tweedie_stanvars()`,
  `tweedie_stan_funs()` (the Stan function block),
  `attach_family_stanvars()`, `log_lik_tweedie()`,
  `check_tweedie_truncation()` runtime diagnostic, and
  `resolve_family_name()` helper for the customfamily naming
  quirk (brms stores the user-visible name on `$name`, with
  `$family = "custom"`).
- `R/make_stan.R` — auto-attaches `attr(family, "mvgam_stanvars")`
  onto the obs setup only, so the trend submodel doesn't get a
  duplicate functions-block / M-data stanvar pair that would
  collide at the final `c.stanvars` merge.
- `R/stan_assembly.R` — `generate_combined_stancode_and_data()`
  now feeds both the obs-side stanvars and the trend stanvars
  into `brms::make_standata()` so the family's data inputs
  (e.g. `M`) make it through.
- `R/posterior_predict.R` — `tweedie` branch in
  `sample_from_family()` (via `mgcv::rTweedie`), plus
  `tweedie = c("mphi", "mtheta")` in `get_family_dpars()`.
- `R/posterior_epred.R` — `tweedie` branch returns
  `family$linkinv(linpred)` (exact: `E[Y | mu, phi, theta] = mu`
  across the full `theta in (1, 2)` range).
- `R/log_lik.mvgam.R`, `R/posterior_predict.R`,
  `R/posterior_epred.R` — `family_name <- family$family` reads
  replaced by `resolve_family_name(family)`.
- `R/how_to_cite.R` — three new `reference_db()` entries
  (`jorgensen_tweedie`, `dunn_smyth_tweedie`,
  `aims_tweedie_brms`) gated by `uses_tweedie_family()`
  predicate.

**Statistical / numerical decisions:**
- **mgcv over the CRAN `tweedie` package** for both `ldTweedie`
  (log-density) and `rTweedie` (random draws): mgcv is already
  in Imports, returns log densities directly, and removes the
  `log(pmax(dtweedie, eps))` two-step that would silently bias
  LOO upward where the raw density underflows.
- **Log link fixed.** Identity / inverse links cannot guarantee
  the `mu > 0` requirement of the compound Poisson-gamma when
  the linear predictor is driven by a latent stochastic trend;
  rejected at the constructor.
- **Series truncation M.** `M = 30` is enough when the implied
  Poisson rate `lambda = mu^(2 - theta) / ((2 - theta) * phi)`
  stays well below `M`. `check_tweedie_truncation()` warns when
  `max(lambda)` at the posterior mean exceeds `0.7 * M`. Default
  is generous for typical-magnitude data; users with very small
  `phi` or `mtheta` close to 2 should bump `M` and refit.

**Verification (2026-06-09):**
- Stan-side state-space recovery on simulated Tweedie + AR(1):
  `mphi` 1.04 vs truth 1.0; `mtheta` 1.53 vs truth 1.5;
  R-hat <= 1.01; 1 divergence in 600 transitions.
- `posterior_epred` / `posterior_predict` / `log_lik` all run
  on the SS fit; `log_lik` is finite for every cell.
- Tweedie vs Poisson LOO on the same overdispersed simulated
  data (var/mean = 1.48): Tweedie wins by 494 ELPD units,
  28.4 standard errors.
- Per-observation log_lik cost ~2.2 ms (600 draws x 300 obs ~
  0.6 s total).
- 62 contract tests in `tests/testthat/test-tweedie-family.R`
  pass.

### 4b. `nmix()` and `occ()` — folded into `jsdgam()` (Step 5)

Original Step 4 also planned `nmix()` and `occ()` as standalone
custom families with their own auto-built `trend_map`. Per user
decision (2026-06-09): both are pushed into the `jsdgam()`
ecology wrapper (Step 5) instead of standing alone on the main
`mvgam()` surface. The trend-map auto-construction, detection-
history layout, and detection-vs-abundance parameter separation
are all ecology-specific and have no obvious cross-domain use.
Royle-Nichols becomes `nmix(type = "royle_nichols")` once
`nmix()` lands inside `jsdgam()`.

This drops Step 4 from "6-8 days, three full families + Royle-
Nichols + cross-cutting predict / pp_check / summary surfaces"
to ~1.5 days (the Tweedie work that just landed).

**Useful patterns to reuse when nmix/occ are tackled inside
`jsdgam()` (recorded here so the wiring isn't rediscovered):**

- **Custom-family plumbing already exists.** `R/families.R`
  has `tweedie()` as a worked example of a brms
  `custom_family` wired into mvgam: `attach_family_stanvars()`
  in `R/make_stan.R` auto-merges the family's stanvars onto
  the obs setup only (the trend submodel must not see them,
  or `c.stanvars` errors on duplicate names); the standata
  merger in `R/stan_assembly.R::generate_combined_stancode_and_data()`
  also has to include obs-side stanvars so any data inputs
  flow through. Both fixes are in place.
- **Family-name dispatch needs `resolve_family_name(family)`,
  not `family$family`.** brms stores `family$family = "custom"`
  on any customfamily and the user-visible name on
  `family$name`. All downstream dispatchers in mvgam
  (`compute_family_epred`, `dispatch_log_lik`,
  `sample_from_family`, `summary.mvgam`, `glance`, `tidy`,
  `how_to_cite`) already route through `resolve_family_name()`.
  New families just inherit it.
- **Custom dpars must be registered in three places.**
  `R/posterior_predict.R::get_family_dpars()` (for log_lik to
  extract them); `R/summary.mvgam.R::match_family_pars()` (for
  the "Further Distributional Parameters" block); and
  `R/index-mvgam.R` `obs_family_pattern` (for tidy / coef /
  parameter classification). Missing any one silently drops
  the parameter from that surface. Tweedie added `mphi` /
  `mtheta` to all three.
- **nmix / occ change the latent structure, not just the
  observation density.** Unlike Tweedie, they need an
  auto-built `trend_map` that links repeat visits / detection
  histories to a shared latent abundance / occupancy state.
  Extend `R/validations.R::normalise_trend_map()` rather than
  routing through the existing `trend_map = ...` user
  argument; document the visit/site shape required in the
  jsdgam roxygen.
- **Likelihood evaluation strategy.** For Tweedie we deferred
  R-side log_lik to `mgcv::ldTweedie` (already in Imports,
  returns log densities directly, avoids the
  `log(pmax(density, eps))` floor that biases LOO upward).
  For nmix / occ no obvious mgcv equivalent exists, so the
  log_lik must marginalise latent N / occupancy in R-side
  code. Plan to write a per-family `log_lik_<family>` helper
  (the existing dispatch in `R/log_lik.mvgam.R::dispatch_log_lik`
  picks them up by name) and a numerically-stable
  marginalisation using `matrixStats::logSumExp`. Confirm
  the upper-bound `K` for the latent-N sum is exposed as a
  family arg like Tweedie's `M`, and that
  `check_<family>_truncation()` warns when the posterior
  pushes the implied latent state near `K`.

## 5. `jsdgam()` ecologist-facing wrapper

**Source:** Heaps factor-architecture plan §"jsdgam translation",
plus the direct user ask in this round.

**Goal.** A wrapper around `mvgam()` that exposes domain-friendly
aliases for joint species distribution model workflows AND
re-ports the abundance/occupancy detection-error families that
were stripped during the brms-integration rewrite.

### Architecture: three layers, three formulas

| Layer | Quantity modelled | Formula slot |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Detection (per visit) | `Y_{s,t,v} ~ DetectionDist(state, p_{s,t,v})` | brms dpar regression: `bf(... , p ~ tod)` |
| 2. Occupancy / abundance state (per closure-unit) | `psi_{s,t}` or `lambda_{s,t}` | existing `formula = species ~ temp` |
| 3. Factor field | `lv_{s,t}` (shared across species via the loadings `Z[s, k]`) | renamed `factor_formula = ~ gp(lat, long) + ZMVN(...)` (alias for `trend_formula` when in factor mode) |

The Heaps loadings_prior machinery sits at layer 2 unchanged.
When env covariates enter `factor_formula` (e.g.
`~ gp(lat, long) + ZMVN()`), factors become explicit
ecological-gradient axes (constrained-ordination /
concurrent-GLLVM pattern); when they only enter `formula =`,
factors absorb residual species co-variation (classic JSDM
pattern). Both are addressable with the same Stan machinery.

### Per-factor env effects — `by = trend` machinery (in scope for v2.0)

Status (2026-06-10): `tasks/notes/jsdgam-design.md` §5b audited
the existing trend-side machinery and found that
`s(x, by = trend)` (and `gp(x, by = trend)`) is **not supported
today**. Trend-side data exposes `(time, series)` columns but
no `trend` axis, and `mu_trend` is per-observation rather than
per-factor (`R/extract_trend_linpred.R:23`,
`R/stan_assembly.R:2515-2517`, `R/validations.R:4776-4805`).
The roxygen example at `R/trend_system.R:2451` advertises the
feature but it does nothing today — silent breakage tracked as
#238. The port itself is task #239 (~170 LOC across
`validations.R`, `stan_assembly.R`, `extract_trend_linpred.R`).

**Why it's in scope for v2.0 jsdgam, not deferred to v2.1.**
Constrained ordination is the headline JSDM feature that HMSC
users coming to mvgam will reach for first. Without
per-factor env effects, `factor_formula = ~ s(elev) + s(precip)`
applies the SAME shared composed smooth to every factor; the
factors then identify a single shared env response gradient
and rotate freely among themselves, so specialisation only
arrives through post-hoc varimax via `ordinate()`. That is
HMSC-incompatible — HMSC's `XRRR` lets the user say "factor 1
tracks elevation, factor 2 tracks precipitation" a priori, and
this is the feature ecologists are actually asking for. Shipping
jsdgam without it would force the very first HMSC migrators to
either accept rotated factors (interpretation gap) or write
their own per-factor formula expansion (DRY break). For vanilla
single-series mvgam the feature is nice-to-have, since most
mvgam users have one or a handful of series and per-series
smooths are an edge case.

**API recommendation (driven by "easy for users to specify").**
The user-facing surface should reuse mgcv's existing
`by =` syntax against a new `trend` factor that mvgam injects
into the trend-side modelmatrix automatically. That mirrors the
already-working `s(x, by = series)` pattern on the observation
side, so the syntax is familiar and discoverable. Worked
example:

```r
# v2.0 jsdgam: factor 1 tracks elev, factor 2 tracks precip,
# factor 3 absorbs residual co-variation (intercept-only).
jsdgam(
  formula        = present ~ 1,
  factor_formula = ~ s(elev, by = trend) + s(precip, by = trend),
  data           = sites_x_species_long,
  species        = species,
  unit           = site,
  family         = bernoulli(),
  traits         = species_traits,
  phylo          = bird_phylo,
  n_lv           = 3
)
```

The wrapper does NOT auto-rewrite formulas; `trend` is a real
factor column injected at fit time with levels equal to the
factor indices `1..n_lv`. Implementation requirements:
  - Inject a `trend` factor column into the trend-side data
    frame at fit time (one row per `(time, series, factor_index)`
    combination); the existing trend-side broadcast already
    repeats `mu_trend` across factors, so this is a data-prep
    extension rather than a Stan-code rewrite.
  - Stan emission needs the trend-side `X_trend` design matrix
    to carry per-factor columns when `by = trend` appears in
    the formula. brms's smooth machinery handles this
    automatically when `trend` is a real data column, so the
    work is plumbing the `trend` column through to brms's
    formula parser at the right point in
    `R/extract_trend_linpred.R`.
  - `R/validations.R::validate_factor_formula()` (new helper)
    rejects `by = trend` for non-factor fits with a clear
    error pointing at the n_lv arg.
  - Removes the silently-broken roxygen example at
    `R/trend_system.R:2451`.

Threading and identifiability interactions documented in
`tasks/notes/jsdgam-design.md` §5b; nothing in the threading
roll-out (#229-#232) blocks this feature.

Tracker: #239 (the implementation) moves from v2.1 deferred to
v2.0 scope; #238 (the broken roxygen) closes naturally when
#239 lands.

### Detection layer comes for FREE via brms dpar regression

Smoke-tested 2026-06-09 against the just-shipped `tweedie()`
custom_family. Confirmed end-to-end pipeline:

- `mvgam_formula(bf(y ~ x, mphi ~ site))` parses cleanly.
- brms emits `b_mphi_Intercept`, `Xc_mphi`, `b_mphi`,
  `Intercept_mphi` parameters and assembles
  `mphi += Intercept_mphi + Xc_mphi * b_mphi` automatically.
- brms calls our custom-family `lpdf` with `mphi` as a vector
  (per-observation) when a sub-formula is supplied, or as a
  scalar otherwise.

**Implication for the jsdgam Stan code**: every custom_family
`_lpdf` we write under jsdgam (occu, occuRN, pcount,
poisson-poisson) must declare its detection-probability dpar
as a **vector** from day one, with overloaded scalar entry
points that broadcast via `rep_vector()` and delegate to the
vector path. This is the pattern Tweedie now uses (see
families.R after the dpar fix). It costs ~10 lines of Stan
per family and gives the user `bf(occupancy_formula,
p ~ det_covs)` for free, with random effects, smooths, GP
terms, and brms autocor structures all working in the
detection sub-formula without any mvgam-side wiring.

So the user-facing call is:

```r
jsdgam(
  formula        = bf(species ~ temp, p ~ tod),         # layers 1 + 2
  factor_formula = ~ gp(lat, long, n_lv = 3) + ZMVN(),  # layer 3
  data           = sites_x_visits_x_species,
  family         = occ(),
  loadings_prior = list(features = traits,
                        distances = list(phylo = phylo_dist))
)
```

### Three deliverables in one wrapper

### Step 5b/c chunk 2 — as-shipped status (nmix("poisson_poisson"))

Status (2026-06-10): shipped in tasks #226 / #227 / #228 / #234.
The `nmix()` constructor now accepts the third variant via
`type = "poisson_poisson"`; PB and RN constructor surfaces are
unchanged. PPM uses log link on both `mu` and `p` (encounter

`ub = c(NA, NA)`. The family name is `"nmix_poisson_poisson"`;

Stan emission (`R/families.R::nmix_poisson_poisson_stan_funs`).
Per-unit log-likelihood marginalises
`N ~ Poisson(lambda)` over `0 : K_max[g]` with the factored
Poisson form:

```
sum_t poisson_log_lpmf(y_t | log(k) + log(p_t))
= log(k) * sum(y) + sum(y * log(p)) - k * sum(p)
  - sum(lgamma(y + 1))
```

The four precomputes (`sum_counts`, `sum_y_log_p`, `sum_p_v`,
`lgamma_const`) are computed once per unit outside the `k` loop,
making the per-k inner cost O(1) after an O(n_rep) precompute.
At realistic `K_max ~ 50, n_rep ~ 5` this is a 5x speed-up over
the naive form; at `K_max ~ 200, n_rep ~ 10` it is 10x. The
k = 0 cell is consistent only with all-zero detection histories
(any_detection guard); Bollen's `1e-9` regularisation is
unnecessary under the log link.

R-side dispatch. Four new kernels in `R/families.R`:
  - `posterior_epred_nmix_poisson_poisson`: closed-form
    `lambda * p` per visit (Neyman Type A first moment is linear
    in both factors; no Jensen correction).
  - `posterior_predict_nmix_poisson_poisson`: two-step generative
    sampling `N_g ~ Poisson(lambda_g)` ONCE per draw per unit,
    then `y_t ~ Poisson(N_g * p_t)` per visit, sharing `N_g`
    across visits to preserve Neyman Type A within-unit
    dependence.
  - `posterior_latent_N_poisson_poisson`: reverse-Bayes
    conditional posterior with the same factored log-weight form
    as the Stan code; same O(K_max) inverse-CDF sampler as PB
    and RN.
  - `log_lik_nmix_poisson_poisson`: per-unit marginal mirroring
    the Stan emission term-for-term.

### Multithreading roadmap (GH #78 explicit ask)

**Status (2026-06-17):** closure-unit families and brms-native
families both thread end-to-end. The brms double-emit bug (#229)
was the real blocker — `threads` arrived on the outer call but
`setup_brms_lightweight()` did not forward it to the mock brm()
call, so brms's `partial_log_lik_lpmf` never reached the stancode.
Fix at `R/brms_integration.R` (forward `threads` when > 1 only;
unconditional forwarding double-emits). `threads` is now also a
first-class named arg on `mvgam()` / `jsdgam()` (#313, 2026-06-17)
with `validate_threads()` early-fail.

brms's `threading()` arg uses `reduce_sum` over the observation
likelihood. For the detection-error families:

- **Standard observation likelihoods (Gaussian/Poisson/NB,
  Tweedie, Bernoulli)**: factorise per observation; reduce_sum
  splits trivially. Wired through `mvgam(..., threads = ...)`.
- **`occ()` / `nmix()`**: likelihood factorises per closure-unit,
  not per observation. flocker explicitly cannot use reduce_sum
  because chunking would split closure-units across CPU chunks
  ([flocker_format vignette](https://jsocolar.github.io/flocker/articles/flocker_format.html)).
  Bollen's RWsim Stan files demonstrate the correct pattern: a
  `partial_sum` Stan function that takes a slice of closure-unit
  indices (NOT a slice of observations) and internally pulls the
  visits for each unit by index. mvgam mirrors that — Bollen's
  `partial_sum` skeleton is now in the `nmix()` / `occ()` Stan
  emission and the closure-unit chunking constraint is documented
  in the `?nmix` / `?occ` Threading sections.
- **Tweedie**: the current per-non-zero `log_sum_exp` over M
  Poisson-gamma terms inside `tweedie_lpdf` is the inner loop
  worth threading. Follow-up after the nmix work shows the
  partial_sum idiom.

### lfo_cv prior pinning (#103 / Task #188) — already correct; docs sharpened

**Resolved 2026-06-09.** Empirical timing on lfo_cv showed the
cache hits already work as intended: 35 refits in 67.9 s
(~1.9 s per refit; cold-compile baseline is ~50 s). Root cause
of the original concern was not a code defect but a stale
mental model. The actual mechanism:

- `mvgam_update_inheritance` (see `R/update.mvgam.R`) inherits
  `prior` from `object$prior` on every `update()` call.
- `object$prior` stores the LITERAL prior table mvgam handed
  to brms at first fit — including the adaptive Intercept /
  sigma literals (e.g. `student_t(3, 50.6, 4.1)` for Gaussian,
  `student_t(3, 1.9, 2.5)` for Poisson, `inv_gamma(0.4, 0.3)`
  for NB shape).
- Because brms sees an explicit prior for the Intercept and
  family aux on the new `update()` call, it skips the adaptive
  regeneration and the stancode is byte-identical → compiled
  Stan model is reused. Confirmed for Gaussian, Poisson, and
  Negative Binomial.

### `methods_md(fit)` Markdown + LaTeX exporter (#187)

**Status (2026-06-17): in design.** Initial prototype tried a
McElreath-style discursive register (data-structure paragraph,
observation-model prose, marginalisation explainer, inference
block, references). Rejected after user review: the function
should emit only the proper mathematical description of the
model and the dimensions of the data, with no prose justifying
choices, no machine terms, and no commentary on what was not
done. `how_to_cite()` already covers the citation surface; this
exporter is the math surface.

**Output shape: three sections, math only.**

1. **Data** — one line, dimensions only. `N = 240 obs, S = 10
   series, T = 24 time points` for the trivial case; extends
   for closure-unit / multi-season fits.
2. **Model** — likelihood + linear predictor + every smooth /
   GP / RE / latent / factor / closure-unit component as math.
   Components stack in strict order (likelihood, linear predictor
   decomposition, each component expanded below, latent process,
   factor block if jsdgam, closure-unit conditional).
3. **Priors** — every prior on the fit as math, no prose.

Sections that were proposed and dropped: Background (user's
scientific question is theirs to write), Marginalisation
(integration is implementation, not the model), Inference
(Stan / cmdstanr / threading is not part of the model
statement), References (handed off to `how_to_cite()`).

**Notation: user-selectable.** Expose a `notation = ...` arg so
users can switch between common conventions (e.g.
`\text{Normal}(\cdot)` vs `\mathcal{N}(\cdot)`, $\beta$ vs $b$,
bracketed-grouping subscripts vs underscored). Default picks the
plainer "text" convention for readability; users targeting a
specific journal pin the alternative.

**Smooths and GPs render as `f(x)` with annotation.** Per
McElreath / standard ecology convention, write
`f_1(season): thin-plate spline, k = 10` rather than expanding
the full basis sum. Same pattern for `gp()` terms — write
`f_2(x): HS-GP, M = 15, length-scale rho, marginal SD alpha`.
Hyperprior lines for the basis coefficients and length scales
still appear in the Priors section.

**Closure-unit notation stays explicit.** Show $z_g$ / $N_g$ as
the latent state and the per-visit conditional below it; do
not collapse to the marginal likelihood. The model statement
is the joint, not the integrated form.

**Multi-response families (`diri`, `multi`, `categ`, `mvn`,
`mvt`) follow McElreath / Gelman convention** — Dirichlet on
a simplex with $\alpha$ parameterisation, Multinomial with
trial-count subscript, Categorical with reference category,
MVN with $\Sigma = \text{diag}(\sigma) L L^T \text{diag}(\sigma)$
Cholesky decomposition, MVT with $\nu$ degrees of freedom.

### Verified Stan parameterizations (from actual stancode dumps)

Use these forms verbatim in the registry renderers. Each was
extracted from a `mvgam(..., run_model = FALSE)` stancode dump
on 2026-06-18 or from `R/stan_assembly.R` / `R/families.R`.

**nl formulas** (`bf(y ~ <expr>, b1 + b2 ~ ..., nl = TRUE)`):
- Per nlpar: `vector[K_<nlpar>] b_<nlpar>`; tparams block sets
  `nlp_<nlpar> = X_<nlpar> * b_<nlpar>`.
- mu computed from the user expression substituting `nlp_*` for
  each nlpar token and `C_k` for the original covariates.
- Math render: one row per nlpar
  `\\beta^{(<nlpar>)}` + a row for the mu expression deparsed
  with `nlp_*` rewritten as `\\beta^{(<nlpar>)}`. Cite #324.

**occ()** (single-season):
- `target += occ_lpmf(Y | mu, p, N_unit, n_rep, Y_max, visit_idx)`
- Internal mixture per closure unit `g`:
  - Σy_g > 0: `log_inv_logit(logit_psi_g) + Σ_j Bernoulli_logit(y_{gj} | logit_p_{gj})`
  - Σy_g == 0: `log_sum_exp(log_inv_logit(logit_psi_g) + Σ_j log(1 - p_{gj}), log1m_inv_logit(logit_psi_g))`
- Math render:
  `z_i \\sim Bernoulli(\\psi_i)`,
  `y_{i,j} | z_i \\sim Bernoulli(z_i \\cdot p_{i,j})`,
  `logit(\\psi_i) = X_i \\beta`,
  `logit(p_{i,j}) = X^{(p)}_{i,j} \\gamma`.

**occ() multi-season**:
- Adds AR(1) on `logit_psi_t` across seasons; see #293.
- Math: `logit(\\psi_{i,t}) = \\rho \\, logit(\\psi_{i,t-1}) + \\xi_{i,t}`.

**nmix("poisson_binomial")** (PB) — verify at Phase D:
- `target += nmix_lpmf(Y | mu, p, K_max, ...)` with truncated
  k-loop. Math: `N_i \\sim Poisson(\\lambda_i)`,
  `y_{i,j} | N_i \\sim Binomial(N_i, p_{i,j})`.

**nmix("royle_nichols")**:
- `y_{i,j} | N_i \\sim Bernoulli(1 - (1 - r_{i,j})^{N_i})`.

**nmix("poisson_poisson")**:
- `y_{i,j} | \\lambda_i \\sim Poisson(\\lambda_i \\cdot r_{i,j})`,
  `\\lambda_i \\sim Gamma(...)` (PPM).

**mvn() / mvt()** (multi-response continuous closure-unit):
- `Y_i \\sim MVNormal(\\mu_i, \\Sigma)` where
  `\\Sigma = diag(\\Psi) \\, L_\\Omega L_\\Omega^\\top diag(\\Psi)`;
  `Psi ~ exponential(1)` is the SD-scale prior already lifted.

**Loadings_prior kernels** (when `n_lv > 0`):
- iid default: `Z \\sim student\\_t(3, 0, 1)` elementwise.
- features kernel:
  `Z[, h] \\sim MVNormal(0, L_\\Phi L_\\Phi^\\top)` where
  `\\Phi = gp\\_exponential\\_cov(features, 1, \\theta_{features})`.
- distances kernel (per matrix `d`):
  `\\Phi \\propto \\exp(-d / \\theta_{dist})`.
- MGP column shrinkage:
  `\\varrho_1 \\sim InvGamma(a_1, 1)`,
  `\\varrho_h \\sim InvGamma(a_2, 1)` for h ≥ 2,
  `\\Psi_h = \\prod_{l \\le h} \\varrho_l` (cumulative product
  via `exp(cumulative_sum(log(varrho_inv)))`).
- Combined: `\\Sigma_h = \\Phi \\cdot \\Psi_h` (multiplicative).

**Trait-mediated loadings** (jsdgam `traits = ~ T`):
- `\\mu_{Z_{s,k}} = T_s \\boldsymbol{\\gamma}_k` plus species RE.

**MGP threshold** (`active_factors`):
- Post-fit `varrho_inv` posterior medians screened against an
  effective-rank threshold (#282); methods_md should note the
  truncation rule when n_lv > observed active factors.
